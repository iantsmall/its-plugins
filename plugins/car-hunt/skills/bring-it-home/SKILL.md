# Bring it home

## Collect at the sale

- The title, signed over by every owner it lists, with the odometer reading filled in and no blanks left.
- A lien release, if the title shows a lender.
- A bill of sale with the VIN, price, date, both names and both signatures.
- From a dealer: their bill of sale, the FTC Buyers Guide and any warranty paperwork.
- Every key, and photos of every document.

## Insure it first

Add the car to the buyer's policy before driving or registering it. New York won't accept out-of-state insurance, and the car must be registered within 180 days of the insurance card's effective date.

## Ship it or drive it

- **Ship it:** get two or three open-carrier quotes (typical costs are in `price-it-out`). The seller has to release the car to the carrier, so agree on timing. Photograph the car at pickup and at delivery.
- **Drive it:** the car needs legal plates for the trip. Dealers usually issue a temporary tag. In a private sale the seller usually keeps the plates, so get a temporary plate or transit permit from the selling state's DMV. Each state's rules differ, so check them before booking the trip. A slower option for New York buyers is to register the car by mail before the trip: the plates and a 10-day inspection sticker arrive in about six weeks.
- **Before a long drive in an old car:** check the fluids, tires, belts and lights; carry basic tools and a jump pack; have roadside assistance.

## Register in New York

- **Bring:** form MV-82, the original title signed over (or the out-of-state dealer's paperwork), proof of New York insurance, and ID.
- **Sales tax:** form DTF-802 for a private sale. For a dealer sale, bring the dealer's bill of sale, plus form DTF-803 if that dealer collected New York tax. If you paid sales tax in the other state, bring the bill of sale showing the price and the tax paid, to get credit for it.
- **Inspection:** when the seller isn't a New York-registered dealer, registration comes with a 10-day inspection extension, so get the car inspected within 10 days of registering it. If you registered while the car was still out of state, the 10 days start when it enters New York.
- **Title:** the new New York title arrives by mail, within about 90 days.

For other home states, look up the same things (tax at registration, the forms, the inspection deadline) on that state's DMV site before advising.

## First week

- Baseline maintenance where the records are thin: oil and filter, other fluids by age, wipers, and a look at the brakes.
- Confirm any open recalls are done.
- In the salt belt, consider a yearly undercoating to protect the rust-free underside you paid for.
- If theft risk is high, add a steering-wheel lock.

Record what was spent in the car's file, and close out the hunt.
