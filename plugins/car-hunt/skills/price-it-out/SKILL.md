# Price it out

The sticker price is the smallest surprise. Tax, fees, getting the car home and the repairs it needs can add thousands, and they decide whether a cheap car far away beats a pricier one nearby.

## Out-the-door total

Build a table with these lines, and mark each one as a quote, an estimate or a guess.

1. **Price,** asking or agreed.
2. **Dealer fees:** doc fee, prep and add-ons. Ask for an itemized out-the-door quote in writing; fees vary by state and dealer.
3. **Sales tax where the buyer registers.** In New York, tax is charged at registration based on where the buyer lives, even for an out-of-state purchase, with credit for tax already paid to another state. Monroe County's combined rate is 8%. For a private sale, the DMV collects it at registration with form DTF-802; report the real price. Re-check the rate, and for other home states look up that state's rules.
4. **Registration and title.** In New York: a $50 title fee, $25 for plates, a registration fee based on the car's weight, and a county use tax ($10 or $20 in Monroe County). Budget roughly $150–250, and check the DMV fee page.
5. **Inspections:** the pre-purchase inspection ($100–250), and the home-state inspection after registration. If the buyer travels to see the car, they can inspect it themselves or use a shop near the seller, which may replace a mobile inspector's fee.
6. **Getting it home.** In KBB's 2026 quotes, shipping on an open carrier averaged about $750 for 500 miles, $1,000 for 1,000 miles and $1,400 for 2,500 miles (re-check). Or price the trip: flight, gas, hotel, time off, and any temporary-plate fee.
7. **Known repairs,** from the inspection and the model's weak spots: a timing belt that's due, tires, brakes, A/C. Price the parts from the buyer's usual source (for Ian, RockAuto), plus their labor: their own time, or a shop's rate.
8. **The insurance change** for the first year.

Add them up, and compare the total with the all-in ceiling in the needs file. When the car is far away, show shipping and the trip as two columns, so the buyer can compare them line by line.

## Fair price

Use comparables: the same model and years, with similar miles and trim, from the same region (the market read from `find-cars`), plus a valuation guide such as KBB or Edmunds. Give a range, never a single number, and adjust it for condition and the repair list.

## Walk-away number

The walk-away number is the lower of these two:

- the top of the fair range, minus the needed repairs
- the all-in ceiling, minus every other out-the-door cost

State it plainly. Don't let the asking price set it.

If a price is already agreed, use the walk-away number to judge the inspection results instead: the gap between it and the agreed price is how much repair cost the deal can absorb before it's time to renegotiate or walk. If there's no ceiling in the needs file, ask for one.

## Reply

Give the table, the total, the fair range and the walk-away number. Add a "Price" section to the car's file.
