# Read the history report

A history report is the best evidence of where a car really lived and what happened to it. It's also incomplete, because plenty of damage and repairs never get reported. Use it to find reasons to stop, not as proof the car is good.

## Run the free and cheap checks first

- **NICB VINCheck** (free, up to 5 searches a day): has a participating insurer reported the car stolen and not recovered, or as salvage or flood-damaged?
- **An NMVTIS report** from an approved provider listed on vehiclehistory.gov, for a few dollars: state title brands such as salvage, junk and flood, the latest odometer reading, and insurance total-loss records.
- **Open recalls** at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
- **Decode the VIN,** and confirm the year, model, engine and trim match the listing.

## Read the report in this order

1. **Title brands and total losses.** A salvage, rebuilt, flood, lemon or junk brand means Stop, unless the buyer wants a project. Watch for "title washing": a damaged car re-titled in another state to drop the brand, often soon after a big flood.
2. **Where it lived.** List each state and roughly how long the car spent there. Years in the salt belt mean you should expect underbody rust, whatever state it's for sale in now. Time in flood-prone areas around storm dates means extra flood checks.
3. **Odometer.** The readings should climb steadily. A rollback or a "not actual mileage" note means Stop.
4. **Owners and use.** How many owners, and was it a rental, fleet, lease or personal car? Long ownership by one person is a good sign.
5. **Accidents and damage.** Look at severity, airbag deployment and any structural damage. Even "minor" can mean frame work, so it goes on the inspection list.
6. **Service records.** Are there big gaps? Is there a timing-belt job, if the engine has a belt? Were fluids serviced on schedule?
7. **Registration gaps.** A long gap can mean the car sat (dried-out seals, old tires, seized brakes) or was off the road after damage. Ask the seller why.

## Reply

Give **Clean**, **Watch** or **Stop**, then the three to five facts that decided it, then the questions to send the seller. Add a "History" section to the car's file.
