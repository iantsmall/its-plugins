# Follow up after the board meets

Read `board/board-profile.md` and the meeting's `minutes.md` and `actions.md`. Follow-up is the step that was missing when board meetings stopped feeling useful, so do it every time.

## Job 1: Put actions in the tracker (right after the meeting)

For each action in the minutes:

1. Check the master task tracker (a Master Plan sheet linked in the profile, if Google Sheets is connected). Do not duplicate an existing row; update it.
2. Add or update a row with: Task, Area, Owner, Status, Due, Next step, Conflict check, and a note "Board decision [date]".
3. Use the next free ID after the last one in the Tasks tab.
4. Mark rows that involve a conflict-of-interest question as "Needs check".

Ask before writing to the sheet. Show the rows first.

## Job 2: Recap email (within 2 days)

Draft, never send, a short email to all directors:

- What was decided (one line per decision)
- Actions: owner, due date
- Date of the next meeting
- A link to the minutes once approved

Use Gmail drafts if connected.

## Job 3: Mid-cycle check (about two weeks before the next meeting)

From the tracker, list board actions that are overdue or due before the next meeting. For each, ask the owner one question: done, on track, or blocked and why. Write the answers into the packet for `prep-board-meeting`. This is a quick nudge, not a meeting.

## Job 4: Email approvals between meetings

Only use this if the bylaws in the profile allow action by email. If they do:

1. Confirm the item is routine and uncontroversial. Anything that needs discussion, anything with a conflict of interest, and anything the bylaws say must be done at a meeting waits for the meeting.
2. Draft one email with: the exact proposed action in motion wording, the reason, the deadline for replies, and the instruction to reply "I consent" or "I object" from the address on record.
3. Count consents against the quorum rule in the profile. Record the result, each director's reply, and the date. One objection means the item goes to a meeting.
4. Put the record in the minute book folder and list it on the next meeting's consent items so it is part of the official record.

## Rules

- Keep one owner and one due date on every item.
- An item that comes back for a third meeting without being finished must be decided, dropped, or split. Say this to the person when you see it.
- Never mark an action done without the owner saying so.
- Do not email anyone without the person's go-ahead.
