# Set up the board profile

Save everything the other board skills need in `board/board-profile.md`. Ask one question at a time. Skip anything already known.

## Step 1: Read the bylaws

If Google Drive is connected, search for the bylaws (title contains "bylaws") and read the newest copy. Otherwise ask the person to attach them. Pull out these rules and save them under "Rules from the bylaws" with the section number beside each:

- Number of directors (minimum and maximum), term length, when terms start and end, and when elections happen
- Minimum number of regular meetings per year
- Notice: how many days, by what method, for regular and for special meetings
- Who may call a special meeting
- Quorum, and how a vote passes
- How ties are broken
- Whether video or phone meetings count
- Whether the board may act by email, and what it takes
- Attendance rule and removal rule
- Officers, their terms, and what each must do
- What the board must see before the Form 990 is filed, and how far ahead
- Whether meetings, minutes and papers are public
- Conflict-of-interest policy: how often it must be reviewed
- How long minutes and other board papers must be kept

Also check whether the copy is signed. If the certificate of adoption is blank, tell the person and suggest finding the signed copy.

Do not guess. If the bylaws are silent on something, write "not in the bylaws".

## Step 2: People

Ask who is on the board now. For each director save: name, office (if any), email, and when their term ends. Then check:

- Are there at least as many directors as the bylaws require?
- Does one person hold two offices? If so, note the bylaw rule about acting in more than one capacity when two officers are needed.
- Who is President (runs the meeting), Secretary (keeps minutes), Treasurer (reports money)?

Do not store anything private beyond name, role and board email.

## Step 3: Rhythm and style

Ask, then save:

- How often the board meets, and which months (the bylaw minimum is a floor)
- Day, time and length of a regular meeting
- Video link or standing meeting room
- How the agenda and packet reach directors (email, shared folder)

## Step 4: Draw the line between board and operators

Boards lose interest when operators do everything anyway. Help the board write down:

- **Needs a board vote:** for example the budget, spending over a set amount, new or changed policies, big projects, loans, contracts over a set amount, conflict-of-interest matters, and filings.
- **Operators decide on their own and report only if something is unusual:** day-to-day care, adoptions, fosters, routine spending under the set amount.

Ask for the dollar amounts. Do not suggest them as if they were facts. Save the result under "Delegation line" and flag it to be approved at the next meeting.

## Step 5: Where things live

Ask and save:

- Where minutes are filed (the shared drive folder)
- Where the master task tracker lives (a link to the Master Plan sheet, if there is one)
- Which email address each director uses for board business (the bylaws may require an address on record for email approvals)

## Output

Write `board/board-profile.md` with these headings: People and offices, Rules from the bylaws, Rhythm and style, Delegation line, Where things live, Open questions. Show the person the file and ask what is wrong. Then offer `plan-board-year`.
