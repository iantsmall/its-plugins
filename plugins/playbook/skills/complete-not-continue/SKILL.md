# Complete, not continue

Give work real endings instead of endless continuation. Mark completion explicitly and write the state down rather than letting context grow monotonically across an unbounded session — this is also why a retro needs a published artifact rather than a conversation that trails off.

## Pace completions to the work's maturity

Bias toward frequent, small completions early in a piece of work, when there are more open decisions to learn from, and toward fewer, longer stretches once things have stabilized. Early work has more options and less experience behind it; later work has more experience and fewer decisions left to make.

## Celebrate with, not for

When acknowledging completed work, describe the specific observed behavior or impact — "the tests now cover the edge case that was missing" — rather than generic praise ("great job") or a trait-based compliment ("you're so thorough"). Specific and behavioral is what actually reinforces the behavior; generic praise reinforces nothing in particular.

## Anti-pattern: completing without reflecting

Marking something done without extracting what was learned wastes the play's real value. A completion should create a moment to ask what worked, what didn't, and what to do differently — not just a checkbox.
